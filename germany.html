Pre-Human History (12 Mya - 600,000 BC)

11.62 million year old fossils of Danuvius guggenmosi, a bipedal great ape, were found in Allgäu, region of Bavaria. The upright walking posture provides a model for the common ancestor of great apes and humans.[3]

Paleolithic (Old Stone Age)

Lower Paleolithic (600,000-300,000 BC)

The discovery of Mauer 1 (Unterkiefer von Mauer) in Heidelberg suggests that ancient humans (Homo Heidelbergensis, named after this found at Heidelberg) were present in Germany at least 600,000 years ago.[1] The Homo Heidelbergensis evolved into both the Neanderthal and the Homo Sapiens. In the Paleolithic site Bilzingsleben (Fundstätte Bilzingsleben), the oldest building of around 400,000 years was found.[2] The at least 300,000 year old Schöningen Spears (Schöningen Speere) are the oldest fully intact hunting weapons ever found.

Middle and Upper Paleolithic (300,000 - 10,000 BC)

Around 45,000 years ago, the anatomically modern human Homo Sapiens (migrated from Africa) arrived in regions inhabited by Neanderthals in todays Thuringa (Thüringen). While the Neanderthals became extinct (around 40,000 years ago), there is evidence that some Neanderthals mated with the modern Homo Sapiens. In the Swabian Jura (Schwäbische Alb), remains were found of 42,000 year old flutes (the oldest remains of instruments ever found), the 41,000 year-old Venus of Hohle Fels (die Venus vom Hohlefels), and the 40,000 year old Lion Man (der Löwenmensch). Remains of Neanderthals that lived around 40,000 years ago (confirmed by DNA sequencing) were found in the Neader Valley (Neandertal).
